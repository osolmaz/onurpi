# 3D quality review

Use this review to decide which visible defects need correction. Judge the requested style and
viewing distance, including any real-world accuracy requirements. Do not replace that judgment with
a polygon count or an invented quality score.

## Review views

Choose enough views to expose the work. Review a standalone object from the front and back. Include
close-ups of its difficult features. Add side or orthographic views when proportions or alignment
are uncertain. Inspect the underside when it will be visible or contains important connections.

For a scene, include the main composition and the places a camera or player can approach. For a
cutaway, inspect the closed exterior and exposed interior from matching angles. For animation,
inspect the extremes of motion and frames where parts pass close to one another.

Use both a readable solid view and a material render when diagnosing geometry. Clear selection
outlines when they hide edges. Check shaded areas under neutral light when shadows or reflections
obscure the surface. Open important close-ups at a resolution that reveals their edges and contacts.
Enlarge the actual render or render a closer view if a contact sheet is too small.

## Shape and construction

Start with the outer shape and part proportions. Compare against the reference before inspecting
surface decoration. Confirm scale with a known dimension when accuracy matters, and separate
measured dimensions from estimates.

Inspect these construction details where applicable:

- Check that holes, recesses, and interior cavities have the required depth.
- Inspect attachment points for floating parts or visible gaps. Look for unintended intersections.
  Measure a gap or clearance when an image cannot resolve it.
- Check wall thickness and exposed cut edges. Keep removable shells separate from the equipment that
  must remain visible in a cutaway.
- Inspect curved silhouettes for faceting at the intended viewing distance.
- Check bevels and shading for pinching or waviness. Preserve the shape of functional edges.
- Examine repeated parts for consistent spacing and correct end conditions.

Topology checks must suit the deliverable. A deliberately open cutaway need not be watertight.
Moving parts need usable pivots and clearances. A still image can justify hidden simplifications
that would fail in a freely explored scene.

## Materials and light

Examine whether each surface reads as the requested substance under more than one useful view. Metal
should respond differently from painted plastic. Check glass against its surroundings and inspect
fabric at the closest intended view. Correct visible texture seams and stretching. Check the texture
scale against the object dimensions.

Judge exposure and light direction before adding decorative effects. Keep enough light to inspect
required details. Check for clipped highlights, crushed shadows, flat lighting, and excessive
emission. Where realism matters, relate interior light to visible fixtures and the environment.
Clearly distinguish a cinematic lighting choice from an accurate physical simulation.

Inspect a plain render as well as the final presentation when post-processing hides form.
Atmospheric effects can support the scene once its geometry and materials hold up under readable
lighting.

## People and activity

Inspect anatomy and pose in close character views. Check the joint shape and clothing thickness.
Check hands against handles or controls. Watch for foot sliding and intersecting limbs. Check that
the body articulates where the motion requires it. Clothing and hair should retain the intended
shape in the poses that will be shown.

Activity should explain the scene. A person repairing equipment needs a plausible position and a
visible relationship to the task. Animation must remain coherent when viewed from an angle other
than the presentation camera.

## Defect record and completion

Keep a short defect list that identifies the affected part and a concrete repair. Include the image
path that shows each issue. For example, "The connector floats above its socket in the rear
close-up; align the mating faces and recheck that view." Distinguish observed defects from uncertain
details that require another image or measurement.

Repair wrong proportions and missing required parts before small decoration. After a repair, compare
the same view and check adjacent areas for regressions. Remove a defect from the list only when the
new evidence shows it is resolved.

Completion requires the requested features and level of finish in the relevant views. Major visible
defects cannot be averaged away by strengths elsewhere in the model. Keep any agreed simplifications
explicit. If the user ends the task or a real limit prevents more work, deliver the current state
with its remaining defects and label the level of completion accurately.
